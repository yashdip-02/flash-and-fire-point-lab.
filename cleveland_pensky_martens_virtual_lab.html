Practical No. 13: Flash and fire point by Cleveland's open cup-apparatus

Course Code: 311305
Course Outcome (CO6)

Use the appropriate engineering material and catalyst appropriately.

Practical Learning Outcome

Determination of the flash and fire point of given lubricating oil using Cleveland open cup apparatus.

Measurement Skills

Adjustment of thermometer suspension and standard test flame sweeping rate.

(VII) Relevant Theoretical Background

1. Practical Significance in Lubrication: Lubricating oil selected for an engine or gearbox must possess a flash and fire point well above its peak operating temperature. If volatile constituents evaporate at working temperature, flammable vapors create extreme fire hazard and thin the lubricating hydrodynamic film, causing mechanical seizure.
2. Flash Point Definition: The minimum temperature at which the oil gives off sufficient vapour to ignite momentarily (a distinct brief flash) when a standardized test flame is passed across the surface under specified apparatus conditions.
3. Fire Point Definition: The lowest temperature at which the liquid produces enough vapor to ignite and continue burning for at least 5 seconds upon application of the test flame. For lubricants, it is typically 10°C to 30°C higher than the flash point.
4. Open Cup vs Closed Cup (ASTM D92 vs ASTM D93): Closed cup testers (Pensky-Martens, Abel) retain light volatile ends inside a sealed vapor space, yielding lower, conservative flash points. Open cup testers allow partial vapor dispersion to simulate open machinery ventilation and can uniquely measure the fire point.

(XVI) Practical Related Questions & Model Answers

Q1. Write precautions to be taken while performing the practical?

• Perform testing in a draught-free laboratory room to prevent premature vapor dispersal.

• Avoid breathing directly over the surface of the oil cup during testing.

• Fill oil strictly up to the internal scribe mark at room temperature (prevent overflow).

• Suspend the thermometer vertically so the bulb is 1 cm (6.4 mm) above the bottom without touching the metallic cup.

Q2. For which type of oil is Cleveland open cup apparatus used?

Cleveland Open Cup (ASTM D92 / IS 1448 P:69) is specifically designated for petroleum lubricating oils and fuel oils having flash points above 79°C (up to 400°C). Volatile fuel oils flashing below 79°C (petrol, kerosene) must be tested using closed-cup apparatus (Abel / Pensky-Martens).

Q3. Explain the procedure to mount the thermometer in the cup?

Hold the thermometer vertically using a laboratory clamp centered midway between the cup's center and side wall. Adjust the elevation so the bottom of the thermometer bulb rests approximately 1 cm (6.4 mm) above the bottom of the cup, ensuring no physical contact with the metal base.

Q4. State the heating rate of oil in this experiment?

The oil is initially heated rapidly at 3°C to 5°C per minute. When approaching within ~28°C of the expected flash point, the heating rate is slowed and held steady at approximately 1°C to 2°C per minute to ensure equilibrium between oil bulk and surface vapors.
